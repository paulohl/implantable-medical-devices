# Clinical-trial expansion: independence and what the trials test

**For Leocadio (2024), DOI 10.5121/ijcsit.2024.02101**  
Checked 28 October 2026 · Seven trial records and 18 coded primary-source passages added. The original pilot and mechanism sheets are preserved.

## Main finding

**The strongest investigator-led randomized evidence supports avoiding transvenous leads, not a clinical benefit caused by AI.** PRAETORIAN and ATLAS were academically led but funded by Boston Scientific. They should not be described as industry-free trials. The Qinming randomized trial reports no sponsorship, although its organizational independence has not been externally established. Four manufacturer-sponsored trials are retained separately as comparator evidence, not relabeled as independent.

No included trial randomizes an AI function on versus off or otherwise isolates a learned model’s contribution. That is the result of this targeted selection—not proof that no such trial exists anywhere.

## Trial evidence that strengthens the paper

| Study | Independence assessment | Clinical result and boundary |
|---|---|---|
| **T01 PRAETORIAN** (2020) | Investigator-initiated; Amsterdam academic sponsor; Boston Scientific-funded. Article reports no funder role in design, analysis or manuscript. Independent event review. | 876 randomized; 849 analyzed. At median 49.1 months, the 48-month composite incidence of device complications/inappropriate shocks was 15.1% vs 15.7%, HR 0.99 (95% CI 0.71–1.39). Noninferiority established, superiority not established. Tests S-ICD versus transvenous ICD in people without a pacing indication—not EMPOWER or AI. |
| **T02 ATLAS** (2022) | PHRI-sponsored investigator trial; Boston Scientific funding. Central expert adjudication documented; full sponsor/analysis arrangements not completely audited. | 503 randomized from 544 screened: major lead-related complications 1/251 (0.4%) vs 12/252 (4.8%); difference −4.4 percentage points (95% CI −6.9 to −1.9). Greater early pain with S-ICD; trial underpowered for shock outcomes. Directly supports the lead-avoidance rationale. |
| **T03 Qinming8631 DR** (2016) | Article reports financial support/sponsorship “Nil,” no conflicts and third-party data analysis. No external verification of sponsor control. | 225 randomized against Talos DR; authors report six-month pacing noninferiority. Per-protocol completion sets were 87/113 and 86/112. Supports conventional pacing performance, not AI. This is an existing corpus source, now given explicit trial-level coding. |

These trials are more informative than a manufacturer announcement for the specific clinical comparisons they make. Their findings cannot be transferred wholesale to an AI mechanism or a combined modular system.

## Manufacturer-sponsored trials: useful, but separate

- **T04 MARVEL 2:** Medtronic-sponsored acute within-patient algorithm comparison. The publication reports 75 people with downloaded software, with a 40-person primary efficacy subset. At least 70% resting AV synchrony was achieved in 95% during VDD versus 0% during VVI. Exposure was up to five hours. This is evidence about accelerometer-based control in predecessor Micra technology, not long-term AI benefit or a direct AV2/VR2 trial.
- **T05 AccelAV:** Medtronic-sponsored single-arm Micra AV study. Of 152 enrolled and implanted, the primary subgroup was 54: mean resting AV synchrony 85.4%, ambulatory 74.8%. An optimized subgroup of 20 reached mean ambulatory synchrony of 82.6%. Do not present that optimized value as the whole-cohort result or transfer it directly to AV2/VR2.
- **T06 AVEIR DR i2i:** Abbott-funded single-group pivotal study, 300 people. Implant success was 98.3%, but the 90-day safety endpoint was 90.3%; **35 serious device/procedure events occurred in 29 patients**. The 97.3% synchrony figure is the percentage achieving at least 70% AV synchrony, not mean synchrony of 97.3%. These distinctions improve the paper’s account of benefits and harms.
- **T07 MODULAR ATP:** Boston Scientific-sponsored, with independent monitoring/event committees but sponsor-performed outcome analyses. The primary report enrolled 293; the six-month endpoint cohort was 162, and 151 completed that visit. Communication success was 98.8% of tests; freedom from major pacemaker complications was 97.5%. There was no concurrent control. **Published online 18 May 2024: this is a later update, not evidence available to the February 2024 paper.**

## Independence is not one label

The workbook separates **academic sponsorship, manufacturer funding, independent adjudication and control of analysis**. Independent event review reduces one source of bias; it does not make sponsor-run data analysis independent. Peer review alone establishes neither funding independence nor independent replication.

The existing SmartECG study is not promoted into this trial table: it is an industry-funded pooled observational/replayed-workflow evaluation, not a randomized clinical AI trial. MARVEL 2 and AccelAV are separate studies; reports within the same AVEIR or MODULAR registry are not counted as new independent trials.

## Verification, dates and access

PRAETORIAN and MODULAR primary articles were read from publicly reachable clinical/hospital PDF mirrors. ATLAS’s primary journal abstract was read through Europe PMC, with funding/governance checked against the investigator’s PHRI presentation and institutional trial page. MARVEL 2, AccelAV and AVEIR were checked against their primary indexed abstracts and ClinicalTrials.gov API records. Their complete articles and supplements were not reviewed. The prior full-text Qinming source remains the basis for its funding statement.

Primary endpoint numbers above come from article prose/abstracts, not flattened PDF tables. For ATLAS, the journal’s P=0.001 is retained in the dataset; the earlier HRS presentation gives P=0.003. These are different source versions and should not be silently mixed.

Current registry enrollments differ from publication cohorts: PRAETORIAN lists an estimated 850; MARVEL 2 lists 77 versus publication 75; AccelAV 157 versus 152; AVEIR 464 versus the 2023 report’s 300; MODULAR 297 versus the first report’s 293. The workbook preserves publication denominators and notes registry counts rather than substituting one for the other. Current records are not historical snapshots of what was publicly posted in 2024.

CiteRight report **1673568** surfaced the original PRAETORIAN report and both trial design papers. Its description of the ATLAS outcome paper as not the original report was inconsistent with the DOI, primary abstract and registry and was not adopted. Its separate AI claim search was unstarted; the conclusion about what was randomized comes from the examined trial methods, not that incomplete search. No exhaustive systematic-review or latest-follow-up claim is made.

Most accessible sources remain copyrighted, not verified open-licensed. Publicly reachable article mirrors and abstracts do not grant blanket redistribution rights. Only short attributed excerpts are delivered. No new full articles are redistributed.

## Coding and use

**Clinical trials** is now the first workbook sheet. **Trial passages** contains 18 quotations; interpretation is separate from quoted text. Codes mark governance/funder role, adjudication, analysis control, trial design, clinical outcomes, follow-up limits and denominator constraints. A registry sponsor field is not itself a financial-funding statement. Unknown or unaudited fields are explicitly labeled rather than filled by assumption.

Use these studies to strengthen component-level safety/performance claims. Keep the paper’s broader AI claim conditional until an AI-specific clinical contrast is documented. Do not pool these endpoints or interpret seven selected trials as a prevalence estimate of independent research.
